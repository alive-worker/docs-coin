Funding Check: A Printed 0.01% Is Not One Annualized Rate

The percentage on a funding page is usually the payment for one settlement interval, not an annualized yield. After the interval moves from 8 hours to 1 hour, the same 0.01% on a 365-day simple basis goes from about 10.95% to about 87.6%. The note uses Binance's settlement clocks and a hypothetical pair of prints. The figures are not a live contract or account, and not investment advice.

ipfsWeb · Published 2026-10-21 14:21:00 Beijing time (UTC+08:00)

Verification checklist

  • ✓At one timestamp, read that contract's funding rate and its own interval in hours. Do not reuse 8 hours from another symbol or from last month.
  • ✓Label the print as one payment per interval, or as already annualized. Until the docs say which, do not treat 0.01% as a daily rate.
  • ✓Simple annualized rate = interval rate times (24 / interval hours) times 365. If the note used a different hour count, that annualized figure is not usable.
  • ✓While two prints still carry their own intervals, do not subtract two 0.01% figures and write that the spread is zero.

1. The printed percent is one settlement, not one year

A common sentence in a note is "funding is 0.01% on both sides." That sentence is missing a clock. Binance's funding-rate introduction defines one payment as notional value times the funding rate, booked on that contract's settlement frequency. An 8-hour schedule settles at 00:00, 08:00, and 16:00 UTC. A 4-hour schedule also settles at 04:00, 12:00, and 20:00 UTC. A positive rate means longs pay shorts. A negative rate reverses the direction. What gets paid is that interval's rate, not the printed number automatically stretched across a year.

The interval is not a permanent property of the contract. The 2 May 2025 settlement-frequency notice says that if a USDⓈ-M perpetual hits the funding cap or floor at the previous settlement, the frequency can move from every 8 hours or every 4 hours to every 1 hour. After that move it does not revert to 8 or 4 hours on its own, and Binance does not publish a fresh announcement for each such change. The read is that symbol's live funding page, or GET /fapi/v1/fundingInfo. Writing "most venues are 8 hours" as a fact about every contract keeps the old multiplier after the interval has already become 1 hour.

This is not the site's other three funding notes. The divergence note asks whether the signal says anything about direction. The cross-venue note asks how margin, slippage, and mismatched settlement clocks eat a raw spread. The snapshot note asks whether the print at the settlement instant was pushed. This note assumes the percentage was copied correctly and asks how many hours that one print covers. The mark-price note asks which series a liquidation engine compares. It does not replace this clock.

2. One printed 0.01%, an eightfold gap in the annualized rate

The teaching case is not a live contract. The printed rate F is 0.01%, or 0.0001. If the interval H is 8 hours, there are 24/8 = 3 settlements a day. On a 365-day simple basis the annualized rate is 0.0001 times 3 times 365 = 0.1095, or 10.95%. If H is 1 hour, there are 24 settlements a day, and the annualized rate is 0.0001 times 24 times 365 = 0.876, or 87.6%. Both screens print 0.01%. The ratio of the annualized rates is 8. On a notional of 10000 quote units, one settlement pays 1 quote unit. An 8-hour interval pays 3 times a day. A 1-hour interval pays 24 times a day. The cash differs by 8, which is not rounding.

The opposite misread is just as common. The 18 September 2025 formula note divides the premium-and-interest expression by (8/N), where N is the interval in hours. At N = 8 the divisor is 1. At N = 1 the divisor is 8, so the printed per-interval rate shrinks in proportion. If the numerator is still 0.01%, a 1-hour interval prints 0.00125%. Multiplied by the real 24 settlements a day, the annualized rate is still 10.95%. A note that instead multiplies that already-shrunk print by the 8-hour count of 1095 gets 0.00125% times 1095 = 1.36875%, short by 8. Remembering "it is 8 hours" fails in both directions: the interval shortened and the formula did not shrink the print, so the annualized rate is understated; or the formula already shrank the print and the note still uses the 8-hour count, so it is understated again. Both paths skipped N.

Compounding moves a smaller number, and it does not hide the factor of 8. Compounding 0.01% every 8 hours for 1095 periods is about 11.57%, against a simple 10.95%. Compounding 0.01% every hour for 8760 periods is about 140.1%, against a simple 87.6%. The interval is an eightfold gap. Compounding is a conversion inside one interval. Align the interval first, then decide whether to compound. Also write down whether the year is 365 or 360 days. This screen uses a 365-day simple rate. A different day count means recalculating, not editing a comment.

3. The interval lives on this contract, not in memory

Three pages can be open at once, so bookmarking one of them is not a read. The introduction lists the 8-hour and 4-hour clocks and describes a default daily interest of 0.03% as 0.01% on an 8-hour interval. The May notice says a cap or floor can move the clock to 1 hour, without an automatic return. The September formula then divides the whole rate by (8/N), so a shorter interval prints a smaller per-settlement rate. Those three pages can annualize one symbol three ways until you have that symbol's interval now, and whether the live formula still contains the divisor.

Comparing contracts needs the same split. Contract A prints 0.01% with H = 8. Contract B prints 0.01% with H = 1. Subtracting them yields 0, which only says the glyphs match. After the simple annualization the pair is 10.95% against 87.6%. The site's cross-venue note still has to count margin, slippage, and clocks that do not settle together. Those costs do not replace this step: before the subtraction, each side is multiplied by its own (24/H). Annualizing only one side can flip the sign of the spread.

4. A screen: the wrong interval means the annualized rate stays out of the note

The teaching screen is ordered. First, the hour count H for that contract at that time comes from the live funding page or from fundingInfo, not from "it is usually 8 hours." Second, a print that is not labeled annualized is treated as one interval's rate. A number that is already annualized is not multiplied by (24/H) again. Third, the simple annualized rate uses this H. If the note's hour count differs from the H that was read, the annualized figure is not usable. Fourth, subtract two rates only after both conversions. If the first three steps fail, writing a spread of 0 between two printed 0.01% figures fails the screen.

The screen does not decide who pays beyond the sign in that venue's docs. Binance's introduction says a positive rate is paid by longs to shorts. It does not say whether the position will be liquidated. That is the mark price and the margin. It does not say whether the rate at the settlement second was pushed. That is the snapshot note. Passing once does not mean the next interval still has the same H. The notice says a move to 1 hour does not revert on its own, so the next interval is read again. This interval's 8 does not multiply the next interval's rate.

5. Keep a record another researcher can replay

The minimum fields are: venue, symbol, read time with the zone written as UTC, the printed rate as copied, whether that print is per interval or already annualized, H, the source of H (live page, fundingInfo, or a notice), settlements per day as 24/H, the 365-day simple annualized rate, a compounded figure only if it was computed, notional and cash per settlement, and the same set for the contract being compared. Two reads on opposite sides of a settlement are two rows.

Reconcile in a fixed order: which page supplied H, whether the print was already annualized, whether the simple rate used that H, and only then the cross-venue subtraction, compounding, and the sign. A note that never reads H stops at "interval missing." If checking that one funding payment actually posted means moving a small amount of margin, put the swap in a fee footnote. It does not belong in the usable-or-not result for the annualized rate.

6. Annualize only after the day has the right number of settlements

The weak sentence is "both sides print 0.01%, so the funding is the same." A useful one is "rate per interval; H; 24/H settlements a day; 365-day simple annualized rate; whether the print was already annualized." Eight hours and one hour are not the same 0.01%. If the formula has already shrunk each print by (8/N), applying the 8-hour count shrinks the annualized rate a second time. Without the interval, the annualized number is an unchecked panel.

This is a research framework. The settlement clocks, the rule that a frequency can move to 1 hour and not revert, and the sign convention that a positive rate is paid by longs to shorts, come from the Binance pages cited above. The figures 0.01%, 10.95%, and 87.6% are a teaching conversion. No live contract was measured, and this is not investment advice.

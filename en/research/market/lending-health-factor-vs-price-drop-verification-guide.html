Lending Check: A Health Factor of 1.5 Is Not a 50% Price Cushion

Aave V3 defines the health factor as collateral value adjusted by the liquidation threshold, divided by debt. A printed 1.5 is that ratio. Subtracting 1 and calling the remainder a price drop moves the teaching line from 2000 to 1500. The note uses the integer rules in that repository's reference implementation to keep the two lines apart. The figures are a teaching case, not a live account or asset parameter on 26 October 2026, and not investment advice.

ipfsWeb · Published 2026-10-26 16:34:00 Beijing time (UTC+08:00)

Verification checklist

  • ✓Weight collateral by the liquidation threshold, then divide by debt. Do not subtract 1 from the health factor and call the remainder a price drop.
  • ✓On chain, 1 is 1e18. Liquidation requires a value strictly below 1e18. A result equal to 1e18 fails the check.
  • ✓0.95e18 switches the close factor: above it, one call repays at most 50 percent; at or below it, one call can repay 100 percent. It is not the liquidation switch.
  • ✓When collateral and debt share a price, that price cancels. Several collaterals need a balance-weighted threshold, not the largest threshold copied onto the whole position.

1. Subtracting 1 from 1.5 does not produce a price drop

After a lending screen prints a health factor of 1.50, notes often continue: "there is a 0.50 buffer, so the price can still fall by half." That sentence turns the spare part of a ratio into a percentage of price. Aave V3 GenericLogic does not compute it that way. Each collateral balance is priced in the base currency, then multiplied by that collateral's liquidation threshold. The denominator is debt in the same base currency. If debt is zero, the health factor is the maximum uint256, not zero. If debt is positive, the health factor is that numerator wad-divided by the debt.

So 1.5 answers "adjusted collateral is 1.5 times debt." It becomes a price drop only under one set of conditions: a single collateral, a debt unit that does not move with that price, and an unchanged liquidation threshold. The health factor is then proportional to price. Reaching 1 requires the price to become 1/1.5 of its current value, a drop of 1 minus 1/1.5, which is one third. The subtraction 1.5 minus 1 equals 0.5, and that subtraction is not in the formula.

This is a different question from the note on borrowing past max LTV. That note compares max LTV with the liquidation threshold: a borrow can already exceed the maximum and still wait until the health factor is below 1. This note stays inside the maximum and asks whether a printed 1.5 is a distance in price. Available borrows use LTV. The health factor uses the liquidation threshold. Substituting LTV into the health factor shrinks the numerator and places the drop too early.

2. Teaching position: the line is 2000, the misreading marks 1500

The teaching position is not a market parameter from 26 October 2026. Collateral is 10 units at an oracle price of 3000, so the base-currency value is 30000. The liquidation threshold is 8000 basis points, which is 80 percent. Debt is 16000, and the debt asset's price does not follow this 3000. Percentage multiplication uses the library's half-basis-point adjustment: (30000 times 8000 plus 5000) divided by 10000 equals 24000. The wad division then takes 24000 against 16000 with a factor of 1e18: (24000 times 1e18 plus 8000) divided by 16000 equals 1500000000000000000. The printed 1.5 is this 1.5e18. Reading the raw integer as "1.5 times 10 to the 18 times safer" misses the scale.

If only the collateral price falls, the price that returns the health factor to 1e18 solves 10 times P times 0.8 equals 16000, so P is 2000. From 3000 to 2000 is a drop of one third. Reading 1.5 as a remaining drop of one half draws the line at 3000 times 1/2, which is 1500. The two marks are 500 apart, one sixth of the starting price. At 1500 the collateral value is 15000, the adjusted value is 12000, and the health factor is 12000 divided by 16000, or 0.75e18. The misreading is not a cautious cushion. It stands 0.25 below the liquidation line.

ValidationLogic sets HEALTH_FACTOR_LIQUIDATION_THRESHOLD to 1e18 and requires the health factor to be strictly below it. At a price of 2000 the adjusted collateral is exactly 16000, the wad division returns exactly 1e18, and the liquidation check fails. At 1999 the collateral value is 19990, the adjustment (19990 times 8000 plus 5000) divided by 10000 equals 15992, and dividing by 16000 yields 0.9995e18. Liquidation starts there, not at the price where the continuous ratio equals 1. In this integer set, the gap of one price unit is the strict inequality.

3. The 0.95 level changes how much one call can repay

LiquidationLogic keeps a separate 0.95e18. It does not decide whether liquidation is allowed. While the health factor is still above 0.95e18, the close factor is DEFAULT_LIQUIDATION_CLOSE_FACTOR, 0.5e4, so one call repays at most 50 percent of the debt. When the health factor is not above 0.95e18, the close factor is MAX_LIQUIDATION_CLOSE_FACTOR, 1e4, and one call can repay 100 percent. The comparison is greater-than, so a health factor equal to 0.95e18 takes the 100 percent branch.

At the teaching price of 1999 the health factor is 0.9995e18, which is above 0.95e18. Fifty percent of 16000, through the same percentage multiplication, is (16000 times 5000 plus 5000) divided by 10000, which equals 8000. One liquidation repays at most 8000, not the whole 16000. At 1900 the collateral value is 19000, the adjusted value is 15200, the health factor is exactly 0.95e18, and the close factor switches to 100 percent, so 16000 can be repaid in one call. Writing 0.95 as "the liquidation line" describes the stretch from 1999 to 1900 as not yet liquidatable. That stretch is already liquidatable. Each call can repay only half.

The same validation also knows a price-oracle sentinel. If the sentinel address is not zero and the health factor is not yet below 0.95e18, liquidation also asks the sentinel whether it is allowed. With no sentinel configured, that branch adds no new price line: the remaining checks run once the health factor is below 1e18. A note that has not read the sentinel address cannot promote 0.95 into a second liquidation line, and it cannot assume the sentinel is on.

4. The price can cancel, and one threshold cannot stand for every collateral

Rewrite the debt in the same asset and the one-third drop disappears. Collateral stays at 10 units and debt becomes 5 units, still priced at 3000. Collateral value is 30000, debt value is 15000, the adjusted collateral is 24000, and the health factor is 24000 divided by 15000, or 1.6e18. At a price of 6000 both sides double: collateral 60000, debt 30000, adjusted value 48000, and the ratio is still 1.6e18. The health factor is unchanged because the oracle price appears once in the numerator and once in the denominator. A sentence that says the price can still fall by a third, or by half, is moving a number that cancelled. What moves the health factor is the debt asset becoming more expensive relative to the collateral, or a change in the liquidation threshold or the eMode category.

Two collaterals also cannot share the larger threshold. The first has base value 30000 and threshold 8000. The second has base value 10000 and threshold 5000. The weighted threshold is (30000 times 8000 plus 10000 times 5000) divided by 40000, which is 7250 basis points, not 8000. With debt of 20000, the numerator (40000 times 7250 plus 5000) divided by 10000 equals 29000, and the health factor is 1.45e18. Using 80 percent for the whole position makes the numerator 32000 and writes the health factor as 1.6e18, which is 0.15e18 too high. When the user is in eMode and the reserve belongs to that category, GenericLogic uses the category's liquidation threshold, not the one printed on the reserve page alone. A nonzero category copied from the reserve page is wrong from the first weight.

Debt is also not the principal on the borrow receipt. Variable-rate debt reads the scaled balance and multiplies by the reserve's normalized debt before the price is applied. After interest enlarges the denominator, the health factor falls even if the price row is unchanged. Dividing adjusted collateral by the original principal overstates the health factor. Stable-rate debt is a separate balance, and both belong in the denominator.

5. A screen: match the units before talking about a drop

The teaching screen is ordered. First, read the raw health factor and confirm the unit is wad, so 1e18 is 1. Second, list each collateral's base-currency value and the liquidation threshold it actually uses. A nonzero eMode category takes the category threshold. The weighted threshold is the sum of balance times threshold, divided by total collateral value. Third, debt uses balances after the index, stable and variable added together, then converted into the same base currency. Fourth, write 1 minus 1 over the health factor as a price drop only when a single collateral price is moving and the debt unit is not. Fifth, liquidation depends on being strictly below 1e18. How much one call can repay then depends on whether the health factor is above 0.95e18. The sentinel address is its own field. If it was not read, stop at "sentinel unknown."

The screen does not say whether a perpetual uses mark price or the latest candle for liquidation, and it does not say whether a displayed APY still has room at this size. Those notes have their own denominators. It also does not redefine max LTV as the liquidation threshold. Passing once means the distance between this health factor and this price is explained by the integer rules above. The index and the price both change, and the next read starts again.

The minimum fields another researcher can replay are: protocol and market, user address, each collateral amount and its decimals, the oracle price, the base-currency value, the liquidation threshold and whether it came from the reserve or from eMode, the same fields for the debt asset, scaled debt, normalized debt, the stable debt balance, the raw health factor, the sentinel address, and the close factor and maximum repayment at that health factor. A note missing the threshold source or the debt index stops at "formula incomplete." Holding a small amount of an asset in order to compare a displayed health factor with the position belongs in a fee footnote. It does not belong in the judgment of whether 1.5 is a price drop.

6. Write the price where the ratio hits 1 before writing what 0.95 can repay

The weak sentence is "the health factor is 1.5, so the price can still fall 50 percent." A useful one is "the health factor in wad; the weighted liquidation threshold; whether debt shares the collateral's price; the price where the ratio hits 1e18; the first price strictly below 1e18; the price at 0.95e18 and the close factor there." In the teaching position those are 1.5e18, 8000 basis points, debt that does not follow, 2000, 1999, and a switch to 100 percent at 1900. The mark at 1500 is the misreading. There the health factor is already 0.75e18.

This is a research framework. The numerator and denominator of the health factor, the strict comparison with 1e18, the close factor of 50 or 100 percent around 0.95e18, and the eMode substitution for the threshold come from the Aave V3 core libraries cited above. The figures 30000, 16000, 8000, 2000, 1999, 1900, and 1500 are a teaching conversion. They were not measured on a live account, and they are not investment advice.
